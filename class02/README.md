## Brief history

From Can machines think? to images that create images, text, video and sound.

1936 Alan Turing, father of computer science, in 1950 he tried to define what is intelligence, he asked whether a machine could behave in a way that appears intelligent to a human.

1956 John McCarthy coined then term Artificial Intteligence, questions they explored:

* Can machines learn from experience?
* Can machines understand and use human language?
* Can machines develop concepts Ann generalization?
* Can machines improve their own performance?
* Can machines use logic to resolve problems?

**Machine Learning:** Approach to AI in which computers learn patterns from data, developed by Frank Rosenblatt (1957). **The key concept:** Instead of programming every rule, we design systems that learn from examples

In 1966 by Joseph Weizenbaum, **ELIZA** was one of the first computers designed to simulate a conversation.

**Rule base Approach:** Follows logical rules

1997 **Machines outperform Humans:** Deep blue vs Garry Kasparov (chess champion world) in chess. Deep Blue was a machine designed exclusively to play chess, it used: rules, calculation, evaluation and decision making.

**Narrow AI:** systems designed to perform one specific task or a limited range of tasks, relies on predefined rules, can outperform humans in its specific tasks, its no self-learning.

**Deep learning revolution:** (2012) Uses artificial neural networks with multiple layers to learn complex patterns from large amounts of data. The more data and layers, the more complex patterns the system can learn automatically.

**Generative AI**:  (2022) analyse, classify, or predict data to generating new content by learning patterns and structures from large datasets.
